# House of Cards — Autoplay Engine Design (Phase 9a)

Status: draft for the director. Nothing here is code yet.

## 1. What changes

Version 1 tracked a story the director wrote turn by turn, with Claude as narrator and clerk. Version 2 is a game that plays itself: houses hold stats and objectives, choose actions from weighted tables, resolve them against dice and modifiers, respond to a deck of real Canadian events, age, die and succeed. The director sets the world going, watches the chronicle, and intervenes when they choose. Prose narration remains Claude's, produced on demand from the event log through the console.

Three principles carry over unchanged: no universal year (every house has a personal clock); land adjacency governs expansion; the database is canonical and every run is reproducible from seed plus log.

## 2. Scenarios

- `scenarios/legacy/` — the reconstructed 2026 playthrough: seed CSVs, house blocks, turns 0001–0002. Used by the test suite and shown on the site's Archive page. Frozen.
- `scenarios/new/` — the live game. Starts with an empty house table and the full 343-riding map. Everything in it is generated by the engine from a seed number and the data tables below.
- `hoc.db` is built from exactly one scenario; the console shows which.

## 3. Time

- One **season** is the engine's tick. Each season every house's personal clock advances one year, every living named person ages one year, and each house takes one action.
- Personal clocks start at 1867 at founding or at accession. They never sync except by an explicit shared event (compact, dispute, challenge, transfer), which sets both participants to the later of the two clocks.
- **Era bands** replace v1's two era cohorts with a general rule: personal years 1867–1899 (Confederation), 1900–1929 (Dominion), 1930– (Late Dominion). Each band has its own climate ledger. A house's climate context is the ledger of the band its clock is in. The old two-ledger structure is a special case of this.
- **The early era is where the game lives.** Every founding and every succession resets a clock to 1867, so at any moment most houses are in the Confederation band, and the band is never "over" for the world. The event deck is densest 1867–1899 and thins after 1929. The alt-history premise that makes this coherent: the Crown in this Canada never stopped granting titles (no Nickle Resolution of 1919), so the peerage renews itself indefinitely rather than fading as it did in Britain.
- **There is no world clock.** The only shared ordering is the season counter, which is bookkeeping, not a date.

## 4. House state

Fixed: surname, peerage title, rank, tag (Progressive / Conservative / Mixed / Outside), cultural community, home province, colours, seat riding.

Variable, each 0–100 unless noted:

| Stat | Meaning | Moves when |
|---|---|---|
| capital | money and productive assets | investments, expansions (cost), events, contraction |
| influence | political reach with government and press | elevations, correspondence, Lead responses, scandal |
| cohesion | internal order of the house | successions, disputes lost, long reigns |
| ambition | appetite for risk (0–10) | set at founding from personality draw; drifts with holder |
| holder_age, heir_named (bool), heir_age | | seasons; naming actions; deaths |
| holdings (count) | | expansion, transfer, loss |

Initialisation at founding: capital = 30 + 5×rank_index + d20; influence = 20 + 5×rank_index + d20; cohesion = 60 + d20; ambition = d10; holder_age = 40 + d30. All draws come from the season's seeded RNG and are logged.

## 5. Objectives

Each house holds up to three weighted objectives. At founding, two are drawn from the table below with weights adjusted by tag and situation; a third may be added by events. Objectives multiply the weight of matching actions.

| Objective | Favoured by | Satisfied when |
|---|---|---|
| Consolidate region | any; more if 1–2 holdings | 4+ contiguous holdings |
| Secure succession | holder_age ≥ 60 and no heir | heir named and aged ≥ 25 |
| Seek elevation | influence ≥ 60 | rank raised |
| Answer a grievance | any Sig− relation | dispute won or reconciliation |
| Form a compact | Progressive / Mixed | ◉+ relation formed |
| Endow an institution | capital ≥ 70 | endowment action succeeds |
| Defend the seat | any incursion suffered | 3 seasons without loss |

## 6. The season loop

For each season, in a fixed house order (by founding, then seat name):

1. Clocks and ages advance. Mortality check for holder (§9).
2. Era events: if the house's personal year matches an event in the deck for its band, the house rolls a response (§8).
3. Action selection: build the list of legal actions (§7), weight them, draw one.
4. Resolution: roll 2d6 + modifiers against the action's target; apply outcome.
5. Objective check: mark satisfied objectives; draw replacements at most once per season.
6. Founding roll: with probability p_found (default 0.15, halved when 40+ houses exist), a new house is founded in an unclaimed riding chosen by region weights and climate fit (§10).
7. Log every draw, roll, modifier and change to the season's JSON record.

### 7b. Enclosure and the late game

A house is **enclosed** when no unclaimed riding is land-adjacent to any of its holdings. Enclosure is checked every season and changes behaviour:

- Expand becomes illegal; Purchase, Marriage alliance, Absorb, Cede/swap and Challenge gain the enclosure bonus above.
- Cultivate influence, Endow and Petition elevation weights double: a house that cannot grow outward grows upward.
- Ambition drifts +1 every 10 enclosed seasons for Conservative and Mixed houses (pressure builds) and −1 for Progressive houses (they turn to compacts).
- Fully surrounded houses that also have cohesion < 40 draw a "Siege" objective: survive 10 seasons without losing a riding, or seek a protector (Marriage alliance / compact) with the strongest neighbour.

When the map is full (no unclaimed riding anywhere), founding stops by itself. The game's engine of change becomes succession: disorderly successions, partitions and extinctions return ridings to play or redistribute them, and founding resumes wherever land reopens. Regional fullness is tracked so that the Prairies can be in a land-rush while Nova Scotia is already in its dynastic phase.

### 7c. Losing ridings other than by challenge

- **Contraction sale:** in a Major Conservative economic event, any house with capital < 20 must sell one non-seat riding to an adjacent house with capital ≥ 50 (or to the Crown, which returns it to unclaimed) at the event's resolution.
- **Debt:** capital below 0 for three consecutive seasons forces a sale as above.
- **Disorderly succession:** in addition to the penalties in §9, a house with 4+ holdings loses its most recently acquired non-seat riding to unclaimed with probability 50%, representing contested wills and Crown review.
- **Cession** through Cede/swap, and **escheat** on extinction (§9). The seat riding is only ever lost by challenge, absorption or extinction.

## 8. Era event deck

A table of real Canadian events keyed to a personal year and a band, each with magnitude (Minor / Significant / Major), tag, and a response table. The v1 record supplies the first sixteen: Confederation Extension 1870, CPR Strike, Smallpox 1885, NW Resistance 1885, Chinese Immigration Act 1885, Boer War 1899, Regulation 17 1912, Komagata Maru 1914, WWI 1914, Halifax Explosion 1917, Spanish Flu 1918, Persons Case 1929, Long Contraction 1930, CCF Founding 1932, Internment 1942, plus one to be identified. Phase 9b fills the deck to roughly forty events through 1960.

Response roll (d6 + tag modifier): Lead, Resist, Exploit, Neutral. Lead: influence +5, climate ledger moves +1 toward the event's tag; Resist: cohesion +5, climate −1 against; Exploit: capital +10, influence −5; Neutral: nothing. Major events also apply a direct effect (e.g. Contraction: capital −15 all houses in band; Flu: extra mortality roll).

## 9. Mortality and succession

Annual death probability for a holder: under 60: 1%; 60–69: 2%; 70–79: 5%; 80–89: 10%; 90+: 20%. Flu and war seasons add a second roll.

On death:
- **Clean succession** (heir named): cohesion −5, clock resets to 1867, heir becomes holder at heir_age.
- **Partition** (two or more heirs named, holdings ≥ 4): the senior heir takes the seat and the contiguous core; the junior heir founds a cadet house of the same community and tag in the outer holdings, with a "kin" relation to the parent. This is how the map's later centuries fill with related houses.
- **Disorderly succession** (no heir): cohesion −25, capital −10, Sig− risk with one neighbour, successor age drawn 35+d20, and the riding-loss roll in §7c.
- **Extinction:** if cohesion falls below 15, or a disorderly succession finds no successor (roll: 20%), the house is removed. Its holdings escheat to unclaimed unless a ◉+ or kin partner exists, in which case that partner may absorb them (Absorb roll at +3). The removed house remains in the record with its full history.

Heirs: the Name heir action can name a second heir once the first is 25+ and holdings ≥ 4; naming a second heir raises cohesion +3 now and enables partition later.

## 10. Founding

- **Season 1:** exactly one house is founded, by the director's choice of seat riding or by the engine's draw if none is given. Everything else follows from the rolls.
- **Founding roll each season:** probability p_found = 0.25 × (unclaimed ridings with at least one land neighbour ÷ 343)^0.5, so the rate is high on an empty map and falls smoothly as land runs out, reaching zero when the map is full. No cap on the number of houses.
- **Region weights** start Maritime 3, Quebec 3, Ontario 4, Prairie 2, BC 1, North 0.5 and shift each season toward regions with more unclaimed land-adjacent capacity, so settlement moves west as the east fills, without any date driving it.
- **Tag** is drawn with climate fit: the current Confederation-band ledger sign doubles the weight of the matching tag.
- **Cultural community** is drawn from a per-region table (Phase 9b authors these with care; names come from community-specific surname banks and never from real living people).
- **Rank at founding:** Baron 70%, Viscount 25%, Earl 5%. **Seat riding:** unclaimed, weighted toward provincial population centres early in a region's settlement and hinterland later.
- **Cadet foundings** through partition (§9) do not use the founding roll and can occur on a full map.

## 11. Determinism and logging

Each season writes `seasons/NNNN.json`: seed, every random draw with its purpose, every roll with modifiers, every state change, and generated one-line chronicle entries. `rebuild.py` replays seasons after seed load; replay must match the committed database exactly. Changing any table is a versioned rules change recorded in `rules/CHANGELOG.md`; old seasons keep the rules version they were played under.

## 12. Director controls (console)

- Run N seasons (1, 5, 10, 25).
- Pause conditions: stop when any of: a house is removed, a challenge occurs, a Major event fires, a house reaches Marquis.
- Intervene: set or veto a house's objectives; force an action next season; grant a house; adjust a stat with a logged reason.
- Rules panel: edit table weights and probabilities; every change versioned.
- Narrate: generate a Claude Code block "narrate seasons A–B, focus on houses X, Y" that writes prose into the chronicle from the logged events only.

## 13. Site

Map with season scrubber; house pages with stat sparklines, objectives, action history; chronicle by season; era ledgers; Archive page for the legacy scenario.

## 14. What stays from v1

Reference data, adjacency, rank ladder, colours rule, transactional runner, exporters, site, tests. The rules engine functions (expand, succeed, elevate, climate_shift, transfer) become the primitives the season loop calls.

## 16. Director's decisions (6 September 2026)

1. One founding house on season 1; all others arrive by the founding roll.
2. Ridings can be lost outside challenge and removal: contraction sale, debt, disorderly succession, cession, escheat (§7c).
3. No cap; the map may fill. Enclosure, purchase, marriage alliance, absorption, partition and extinction carry the late game (§7b, §9).
4. The early era is maximised structurally: personal clocks always restart at 1867, the deck is densest before 1900, and the Crown never stops granting titles.

## 17. Sanity targets for the 9c smoke test

Over 300 seasons from one house: the map should reach 80% claimed somewhere between seasons 90 and 200; the house count should peak between 45 and 90 and then oscillate rather than collapse; at least one partition, one absorption and one extinction should occur; no stat leaves 0–100; no house is enclosed for more than 60 seasons without an action from §7b resolving it. Tables are tuned until these hold across ten seeds.
